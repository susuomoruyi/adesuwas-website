How We Got Our First 1,000 Customers at Accrue

Before I joined Helicarrier, I worked as a teacher at a primary and secondary school in Ogun State, earning ₦18,000 a month. That was from late 2020 to around March 2021. The money wasn’t much, and I didn’t enjoy the job, but I hated the idea of staying at home even more. Apart from not wanting to sit idle, I was also worried about running out of the little money I’d saved up in university if I didn’t have any new inflow.

In March 2021, I joined Helicarrier, where I met my co-founder, Zino. I started out as a customer success associate, but after some time, I became interested in marketing. I liked the idea of helping a company grow, seeing more people use a product, and figuring out what makes people care about it. When I told my employers, they let me move to marketing.

A few months after joining Helicarrier, around May 2021, Zino told me about an idea he wanted to work on — a dollar-cost-averaging app that would help people invest in crypto and stocks (this was the first version of Accrue, before our multiple pivots). He asked if I wanted to work on it as a side hustle, and I said yes immediately.

At that time, I had just finished my final University exams and was waiting for graduation (this was delayed by a year due to COVID). I was 21, living with my brother, and didn’t really have anything to lose. I was just excited to try something new.

Working at Helicarrier was great, but the company already had a solid user base. I wanted to experience what it was like to start from scratch and try to grow something from the ground up. That challenge felt exciting to me; that’s why it was an immediate yes when Zino asked.

When we were preparing to launch, two people from Helicarrier — Teju and Tomiwa — gave us an idea that changed how we approached our beta launch. They suggested adding a dollar-cost-averaging calculator to the website. The calculator showed people how much they would have made if they had been investing small amounts, such as $5 every week, in Bitcoin or Apple stock since a specific year. When users saw their results, they could click a button to share them on Twitter, with a link back to the site so others could check theirs too.

That small idea worked really well.

Around the same time, I was supposed to go to NYSC camp, but I didn’t want to because I felt it would distract me from what we were building. My parents didn’t like that, so after losing all the arguments with them, I went. I ended up staying for just two days because I fell sick. I was happy to get back just in time to focus on this.

When we finally launched the calculator in September 2021, I remember seeing the first few people share their results on Twitter. It felt so unreal. People were actually using something we built.

Within a few weeks, we had about a thousand people on our waitlist. The app was still in its MVP phase, so we couldn’t let everyone in at once. We started giving access in small batches.

Looking back, I think the calculator was effective because it piqued people's curiosity. Everyone wanted to see how much they could have made if they’d started investing earlier. It also created FOMO; that feeling of not wanting to miss out again. And when people shared their results, others saw them and wanted to check theirs too.

That’s how we got our first 1,000 users at Accrue. Not by running ads or doing anything complex, but by creating something simple that made people curious enough to share it.

And maybe the biggest lesson here is to start with what you have. We didn’t have a big team or a big budget. We just had this little idea our friends shared with us, and it turned out to be exactly what we needed.
